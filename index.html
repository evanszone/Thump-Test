Before you thump

How to thump
  • Set the melon on the cart or cradle it in one arm. Use the same setup for every melon.
  • Hold your phone about 4 inches from the rind.
  • Knock firmly with a knuckle 3–5 times, about half a second apart, at the middle of the melon.
  • Step away from freezers and speakers if you can. Background noise blurs the reading.
How it works

Each knock makes the melon ring at its own resonant pitch. As a watermelon ripens, its flesh softens and that pitch drops. The app finds every knock in your recording, measures the strongest tone and how long it rings, and takes the middle value across your knocks.

With weights, it ranks by a stiffness index (pitch² × mass^⅔) that agricultural researchers use, so size doesn't skew the result. A melon that sounds very deep but goes dead almost instantly can be past its prime, so the app flags it instead of picking it.

It compares the melons in front of you. It can't promise one is sweet on its own.

Check with your eyes too
  • A creamy yellow field spot (where it sat on the ground) is good. White means it was picked early.
  • It should feel heavy for its size.
  • A dull rind beats a shiny one, and brown webbing scars often mean extra sugar.

Recordings are analyzed on your phone and never leave it.
